Exercise 4: Nested loop threading, with and without collapse(2)

Without collapse(2):
#pragma omp parallel for only parallelizes the OUTER loop (r, 5 values).
Only 5 threads (0-4) were used, one per value of r; each thread then
executed its entire inner loop (all 10 values of k) sequentially, by
itself. Even though 12 threads/cores were available, 7 sat idle -
the work was capped at 5-way parallelism because that is all the outer
loop offers.

With collapse(2):
#pragma omp parallel for collapse(2) merges the two nested loops into a
single logical loop of 5 x 10 = 50 iterations, and OpenMP distributes
ALL 50 (r,k) pairs across however many threads are available. This run
used all 12 threads (0-11), each handling only about 4 iterations. The
iterations assigned to a single thread are no longer confined to one
value of r - e.g. thread 4 handled both (r=2, k=9-10) and (r=3, k=1-2),
crossing the r boundary, showing the two loops were truly merged into
one iteration space before being divided among threads rather than
being split loop-by-loop.

Conclusion: collapse(2) is useful when the outer loop alone does not
have enough iterations to keep all available threads busy (5 outer
iterations vs 12 threads here) - collapsing exposes the full 50-way
parallelism of the nested loops instead of being limited to 5-way.
